Explore a different Tarot tradition

Tarot de Marseille

The Tarot de Marseille is a family of historical 78-card patterns. Its bold, traditional images and unillustrated numbered suit cards invite a different approach from the Rider–Waite–Smith deck.

What makes Marseille different?

The Marseille tradition has 22 trumps and four suits of 14 cards: Cups, Coins (often called Pentacles in English), Swords and Batons (often called Wands). The court ranks are Valet, Cavalier, Reine and Roi. English-language guides often map these to Page, Knight, Queen and King.

Many Marseille decks use VIII for Justice and XI for Strength (La Force). This is the reverse of the order used in the familiar Rider–Waite–Smith deck. The Fool is often called Le Mat, while the unnamed thirteenth trump is commonly referred to as L’Arcane sans Nom.

How to read the numbered suit cards

Unlike Rider–Waite–Smith, traditional Marseille pip cards do not normally show a fully illustrated scene for each number. The reader combines the number's developmental quality with the suit's area of life, then studies the arrangement of the emblems, symmetry, colours and decorative details.

  • Cups: emotional exchange, relationships and belonging.
  • Coins / Pentacles: resources, work, tangible results and practical life.
  • Swords: thought, conflict, discernment and decisions.
  • Batons / Wands: energy, enterprise, creativity and action.

Number symbolism is not a rigid formula. Different Marseille readers and historical sources interpret pip patterns in different ways, so treat the guide meanings as one modern learning approach rather than the only correct system.

Reversals are optional

Some readers use reversed cards in Marseille readings; others read every card upright and let the surrounding cards, question and visual pattern supply nuance. Our free reading tool lets you include or exclude reversals. The reversed interpretations in these guides are optional prompts, not a universal historical rule.

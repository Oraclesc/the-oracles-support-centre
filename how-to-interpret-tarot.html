How to Interpret Tarot Cards

Learning the traditional meaning of a tarot card is only the beginning. A useful interpretation also considers the question being asked, the imagery on the card, its position in a spread and the circumstances of the person receiving the reading.

This guide explains a simple way to bring those elements together without feeling that there is only one correct answer for every card.

A Card Does Not Have Only One Meaning

Tarot cards have traditional themes, but a card is not a dictionary entry that always means exactly the same thing. The same card can feel very different depending on the question, the position it occupies and the cards surrounding it.

Think of a card meaning as a starting point rather than a final answer. Your task as a reader is to explore how that starting point connects with the situation in front of you.

1

Start With the Question

Before interpreting a card, return to the question that brought you to the reading.

A card should be interpreted in relation to something. Without a question or area of focus, it can be easy to attach a random meaning to whatever you happen to notice.

For example, The Hermit in a reading about career may encourage you to consider independence, learning or taking time to work out what you actually want.

The same card in a reading about a relationship could instead draw attention to personal space, solitude or the need to understand your own feelings.

The card has not changed. The context has.

2

Observe the Image Before Looking Up the Meaning

Take a moment to look at the card itself.

Notice the people, objects, expressions, landscape, movement, direction and atmosphere.

Ask yourself:

  • What did I notice first?
  • What emotion does the image create?
  • Does the scene feel active or still?
  • Is anyone moving towards something or away from it?
  • Is there an object that seems particularly important?
  • Does anything remind me of the situation I asked about?

This does not mean that every visual detail has a secret meaning. Observation simply gives you a personal starting point before you compare your impression with traditional interpretations.

3

Consider the Traditional Meaning

Once you have formed your first impression, consider the recognised themes of the card.

Traditional meanings provide a useful foundation. They help prevent an interpretation from becoming completely disconnected from the established symbolism of the deck.

At the same time, you do not need to force every keyword into every reading.

If a card has ten commonly associated themes, the question may only make two or three of them particularly relevant.

4

Look at the Position of the Card

In a tarot spread, the position of a card gives it another layer of meaning.

Imagine a three-card spread with the positions:

  1. What is influencing the situation?
  2. What should I understand now?
  3. What might I consider next?

The same card appearing in each position would not necessarily be interpreted in exactly the same way.

The position tells you what role the card is playing within the reading.

5

Consider the Cards Around It

Tarot becomes particularly interesting when several cards are read together.

Instead of interpreting every card as a completely separate message, look for relationships between them.

You might notice:

  • Several cards sharing a similar emotional theme.
  • Repeated numbers or visual patterns.
  • Several cards from the same suit.
  • A movement from uncertainty towards clarity.
  • A tension between two different ideas.

The goal is not to force a dramatic story. Simply notice whether the cards appear to reinforce, challenge or develop one another.

6

Bring the Meaning Back to Real Life

After considering the symbolism, traditional meaning, question and spread position, bring the interpretation back to the person's actual circumstances.

Ask:

  • Where does this theme appear in my situation?
  • What part of this message feels relevant?
  • Is there something I have been avoiding?
  • What is within my control?
  • What could I approach differently?

This is where a reading becomes more than memorising keywords. The interpretation becomes connected to a real question.

✨

Where Does Intuition Fit?

Intuition can be part of tarot reading without meaning that every spontaneous thought must be treated as a hidden message.

Sometimes a particular image, expression or detail simply catches your attention. Take note of it and ask yourself why it stood out.

Your intuition can then be considered alongside the traditional meaning rather than replacing it entirely.

Over time, keeping a tarot journal can help you notice which impressions consistently feel useful and which ones may have been influenced by the mood of the moment.

Interpreting Reversed Cards

A reversed card does not automatically mean something negative.

Depending on the reader and the context, a reversal might suggest blocked energy, an internal experience, something delayed, an imbalance or a different expression of the card's usual theme.

The important thing is consistency. If you choose to work with reversed cards, develop an approach that you understand and can apply without automatically treating every reversal as bad news.

A Simple Worked Example

Imagine someone asks:

"What should I focus on as I begin something new?"

They draw The Fool.

A basic keyword interpretation might say "new beginnings". That is a useful starting point, but we can go further.

The image suggests movement, openness and stepping into unfamiliar territory. The traditional meaning supports themes of beginnings, curiosity and taking a leap of faith.

The question asks what the person should focus on, rather than what is guaranteed to happen.

The interpretation might therefore become:

Approach the new experience with curiosity and openness, while remembering that taking a chance does not require ignoring sensible preparation.

Notice the difference. The card has not been treated as a prediction. Its themes have been connected with the question to produce something the person can actually reflect on.

✦

A Simple Interpretation Method

When you are unsure what a card means, use this sequence:

  1. Question: What am I actually asking?
  2. Observe: What catches my attention in the image?
  3. Meaning: What are the card's traditional themes?
  4. Position: What role does the card have in this spread?
  5. Connection: How does it relate to the other cards?
  6. Reflection: What could I take from this into my situation?

With practice, these steps become less mechanical. Eventually you may find yourself moving naturally between imagery, traditional meanings and your own observations.

The Goal Is Understanding, Not Prediction

A good tarot reading does not need to produce a dramatic prediction. Sometimes the most useful result is simply seeing a familiar situation from a perspective you had not considered.

Use the cards as an invitation to slow down, ask better questions and think carefully about what is within your control.

Continue learning, explore the cards and develop an approach that feels natural to you.

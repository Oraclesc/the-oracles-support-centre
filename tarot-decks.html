Find a deck that works for you

Tarot Deck Library

A good deck is one you can comfortably read. Compare structure, artwork, symbolism, guidebook quality and physical feel rather than choosing only by appearance.

Rider–Waite–Smith

The visual tradition used throughout this site. The fully illustrated Minor Arcana makes it especially approachable for learning symbolism and spreads.

Tarot de Marseille

A historic European tradition. The numbered Minor Arcana are less scene-based, encouraging readers to work with suit, number and pattern.

Thoth tradition

A modern occult Tarot system associated with dense symbolic and astrological correspondences. It rewards slower, structured study.

Modern RWS-inspired decks

Many contemporary decks retain the familiar 78-card structure while changing the artwork, theme, representation or visual language.

Independent & self-published decks

These can offer distinctive art and themes. Check the creator's guidebook, card dimensions, printing quality and whether the deck follows a system you recognise.

Digital Tarot decks

Useful for practice on the move. If learning seriously, keep a written record so the convenience of an app does not replace close observation of the cards.

Deck comparison checklist

Structure

Does it contain 78 cards? Are the Major and Minor Arcana organised clearly? Are courts named in a way you understand?

Artwork

Can you distinguish expressions, objects, colours and actions easily? Do the images give you enough to observe?

Guidebook

Does it explain the system, spreads and card meanings clearly enough for your learning level?

Physical feel

Consider card size, finish, flexibility, print quality and whether you can shuffle comfortably.

Choosing your first deck

If you are new to Tarot, a deck that follows a familiar system can make learning resources easier to use. That does not mean another system is wrong — it simply changes the learning path.

A Brief History of Tarot

Tarot has a longer and more complicated history than many modern descriptions suggest. This guide separates documented developments from later occult traditions and popular origin stories.

1

Tarot's Italian Beginnings

The earliest surviving Tarot-like packs date from 15th-century northern Italy. They were luxury card packs associated with courtly culture and games. This matters because Tarot was not originally created as a fortune-telling system.

2

From Trionfi to Tarot

Early Italian packs included an additional sequence of special cards alongside four ordinary suits. Names, imagery and card counts varied between early decks. The modern 78-card structure developed through the history of these traditions rather than appearing fully formed at one moment.

3

Playing Cards Before Divination

For much of Tarot's early history, the cards were used for games. The association between Tarot and divination appeared later. This is one reason it is useful to distinguish documented card history from stories about an ancient origin.

4

The Marseille Tradition

From the 17th century onward, patterns of Tarot imagery associated with French and later European printing traditions became influential. The term “Tarot de Marseille” is now commonly used for a family of related historical designs rather than one single original deck.

5

Occult Tarot in the 18th and 19th Centuries

Writers including Antoine Court de Gébelin and Jean-Baptiste Alliette (Etteilla) helped establish ideas about Tarot as a divinatory and esoteric system. Later occult traditions added correspondences with astrology, Kabbalah and other symbolic frameworks.

6

Ancient-Origin Claims

Stories linking Tarot directly to ancient Egypt, secret priests or a lost ancient wisdom tradition became influential in occult writing. These claims are part of Tarot's later cultural history, but they are not established as the historical origin of the cards.

7

The Rider–Waite–Smith Deck

The Rider–Waite deck was published in 1909. Arthur Edward Waite developed the system and Pamela Colman Smith created the illustrations. Its illustrated Minor Arcana became especially influential because the scenes gave readers visual material beyond simple suit-and-number patterns.

8

Pamela Colman Smith's Contribution

Smith's artwork helped define the visual language familiar to many modern readers. Her scenes use gesture, setting, objects and composition to create small visual narratives, making the Minor Arcana more accessible for interpretive reading.

9

Tarot in the 20th and 21st Centuries

Tarot expanded through new decks, books, publishers, artists and reading communities. Modern decks may closely follow older traditions or reinterpret the structure through different cultures, artistic styles and themes.

10

Tarot Today

Today Tarot can be approached as a historical card tradition, game, artistic medium, spiritual practice or reflective tool. There is no single modern interpretation shared by every reader. Learning the history gives you context without requiring you to adopt one particular belief system.

11

Why the History Matters

Knowing where ideas came from helps separate evidence from later tradition. It also explains why two readers can use the same 78 cards but disagree about meanings, reversals, correspondences or divination. Tarot has always been a changing cultural practice.

12

A Simple Historical Timeline

15th century: early Italian Tarot-like packs. 16th–17th centuries: Tarot develops as a European card-game tradition and printing patterns change. 18th century: documented divinatory and occult interpretations emerge. 19th century: esoteric systems become more elaborate. 1909: Rider–Waite is published. 20th–21st centuries: Tarot becomes a global artistic, spiritual and reflective practice.

🧭

Historical Sources and Further Study

For deeper research, compare museum collections, surviving historical decks and specialist scholarship rather than relying on a single modern Tarot guide. Historical evidence is strongest when claims can be connected to dated cards, documents, publications or documented practices.

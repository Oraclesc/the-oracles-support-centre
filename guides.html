Tarot Guides for Beginners

Tarot can look complicated when you first encounter a 78-card deck, but learning it does not have to happen all at once. These guides are designed to help you understand the structure of tarot, develop your own way of interpreting cards and approach readings with curiosity rather than pressure.

The Oracle's Support Centre takes a reflective approach to tarot. The cards are not presented as a way to guarantee future events. Instead, they can be used as prompts for reflection, perspective and thoughtful questions.

New to Tarot? Start Here

If you are holding a tarot deck for the first time, you do not need to memorise all 78 cards before you begin. Start by understanding how the deck is organised, then practise reading one card at a time.

A simple learning path is:

  1. Learn the difference between the Major and Minor Arcana.
  2. Become familiar with the four Minor Arcana suits.
  3. Learn a few simple spreads rather than trying every spread at once.
  4. Ask questions that invite reflection rather than demanding a guaranteed prediction.
  5. Keep notes about your readings and return to them later.

Most importantly, allow yourself to be a beginner. Tarot becomes easier through repeated observation and practice, not through trying to become perfect immediately.

How to Read Tarot

Learning to read tarot does not mean memorising all 78 cards. A reading begins with a clear question, a simple spread and a willingness to look closely at what the cards suggest.

This beginner-friendly guide walks through the basic process of choosing a question, drawing cards, understanding their positions and connecting the cards into a meaningful reading.

The aim is not to predict the future with certainty. Instead, tarot can provide a structured way to reflect on situations, possibilities, choices and different perspectives.

What Is Tarot?

Tarot is a deck of 78 illustrated cards traditionally divided into the Major Arcana and Minor Arcana. Readers use the imagery, symbolism and traditional meanings of the cards to explore questions and situations.

A tarot reading can be approached in many different ways. Some readers concentrate heavily on established card meanings, while others pay more attention to imagery, personal associations and intuition.

There is no requirement to treat every card as a fixed prediction. A useful reading can instead ask: What does this card make me notice? What possibility have I overlooked? What might I approach differently?

Understanding the Tarot Deck

A standard tarot deck contains 78 cards. Twenty-two cards form the Major Arcana, while 56 cards form the Minor Arcana.

The Major Arcana often deals with larger themes, transitions, lessons and turning points. The Minor Arcana tends to focus more closely on everyday experiences, emotions, choices and practical situations.

The Minor Arcana is divided into four suits: Cups, Pentacles, Swords and Wands. Each suit gives another way of looking at a situation.

How to Ask Better Tarot Questions

The quality of a reading often begins with the quality of the question. Questions that allow exploration usually give you more to work with than questions that demand a simple yes or no.

For example, instead of asking “Will I get the job?” you might ask “What should I understand about my approach to this opportunity?” The second question creates room for reflection and action.

Good tarot questions can focus on your choices, perspective, preparation, relationships, habits and possible next steps.

Choosing a Tarot Spread

A spread gives each card a specific position or role within a reading. The position helps determine what part of the question the card is exploring.

Beginners often find one-card and three-card readings easier to understand than large spreads. A simple three-card spread might explore a situation, what is influencing it and a possible way forward.

More cards do not automatically create a better reading. A smaller spread can sometimes make the message easier to understand because there are fewer relationships to interpret.

Understanding Reversed Tarot Cards

A reversed card is simply a card that appears upside down in a reading. Different readers interpret reversals in different ways.

A reversal does not automatically mean that a card's meaning becomes negative. It can suggest blocked energy, an internal experience, something delayed, an imbalance or a different expression of the card's theme.

You can also choose not to use reversed cards at all. What matters most is having a method that you understand and can apply consistently.

Reading Tarot Symbolism

Tarot imagery contains recurring colours, objects, animals, landscapes, numbers and figures. Paying attention to these details can help you move beyond simply memorising keywords.

When looking at a card, notice what immediately attracts your attention. Look at the direction figures are facing, the environment around them and the objects they are holding.

Symbolism can become especially useful when several cards in the same reading appear to share colours, numbers, elements or visual themes.

Building a Daily Tarot Practice

You do not need an elaborate ritual to practise tarot. One card each day can be enough.

Draw a card in the morning and write down your first impressions. During the day, notice whether the card changes what you pay attention to. Later, return to your notes and consider what you noticed.

Over time, this gives you a personal record of how you understand different cards in different situations. It can also help you recognise which interpretations feel useful to you.

Keeping a Tarot Journal

A tarot journal turns individual readings into a record that you can learn from. You can record the date, question, cards drawn, your first impressions and what eventually happened or became clearer.

Try writing your interpretation before looking up a traditional meaning. Afterwards, compare the two. This can show you where your personal observations already align with established meanings and where you might want to explore further.

Reading Tarot Card Combinations

A spread is more than a collection of individual card meanings. Cards influence one another through their positions, themes and visual relationships.

Look for repeated suits, numbers, elements and emotional themes. Then ask how one card changes the way you understand another.

For example, several cards connected with movement may suggest that change is an important theme, while several cards connected with pause or reflection may encourage a slower approach.

Common Beginner Mistakes

Learning tarot can become unnecessarily difficult when every reading is treated as a test that must have a perfect answer.

Common problems include repeatedly asking the same question because the first answer was uncomfortable, trying to force every card into a fixed meaning and using increasingly complicated spreads before becoming comfortable with simple ones.

Another useful habit is to separate what the card actually shows from what you hope or fear it means. This can make readings more balanced and reflective.

Developing Tarot Intuition

Intuition does not have to mean ignoring traditional meanings. You can use both approaches together.

Before looking up a card, describe what you notice. What emotion does the image create? What seems to be happening? Which detail catches your eye first?

Then compare your observations with the established meaning of the card. With practice, the two approaches can begin to work together.

Tarot Myths and Facts

Tarot has accumulated many traditions and myths. Some are personal practices, some come from particular schools of tarot and others are simply popular misconceptions.

You do not have to follow every tradition you encounter. Learning where different practices come from can help you decide which approach feels appropriate for your own readings.

Tarot Is a Tool for Reflection, Not Prediction

The approach used throughout The Oracle's Support Centre is intentionally reflective. A card can offer a different way of looking at a situation, but it does not remove your responsibility for the decisions you make.

Instead of asking a reading to decide something for you, consider using it to explore possibilities. What are you overlooking? What assumption might be influencing you? What choice deserves more thought?

This approach can make tarot useful even when you are not interested in treating the cards as predictions of fixed future events.

A Simple Reading Process

When you are ready to practise, keep the process simple:

  1. Take a moment to settle your attention.
  2. Decide what you want to explore.
  3. Choose a suitable spread.
  4. Look at the cards before immediately checking their meanings.
  5. Consider the position of each card.
  6. Look for connections between the cards.
  7. Write down your interpretation.
  8. Decide what, if anything, you want to do with the perspective you gained.

The goal is not to produce a dramatic answer. A good reading can sometimes simply help you notice a question you had not considered before.

Reading for Yourself

Reading for yourself can be useful because you already know the situation being explored. It can also make it easier to project hopes or fears onto a card.

One way to reduce this effect is to write your initial interpretation before deciding whether you like the answer. Returning to the reading later can also provide useful distance.

If a reading makes you anxious, repeatedly checking the cards for reassurance may not be helpful. Sometimes the most useful response is to put the cards away and return to the real-world situation with a clearer mind.

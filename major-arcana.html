The Major Arcana Tarot Cards

The Major Arcana is made up of 22 cards that represent important lessons, turning points, personal growth and powerful life experiences. Together, they tell a symbolic story often known as The Fool's Journey.

0

The Fool

Upright: New beginnings, freedom, curiosity, adventure and taking a leap of faith.

Reversed: Recklessness, hesitation, poor planning or fear of beginning something new.

The Fool represents the beginning of a journey. It encourages openness, trust and a willingness to step into the unknown.

The Fool in a Reading

The Fool can appear when you are standing at the beginning of something unfamiliar. It may suggest curiosity, possibility and the willingness to learn through experience rather than knowing every answer beforehand.

The card does not necessarily mean that you should act without thinking. Its invitation to take a leap of faith can be balanced by awareness of the risks involved and a willingness to learn from what happens next.

The Fool as Advice

Consider where you may be holding yourself back simply because you cannot predict the outcome. The Fool can encourage you to approach a new experience with curiosity while still using sensible judgement.

Questions for Reflection

  • What new experience am I being invited to explore?
  • Where could I benefit from being more open-minded?
  • Am I taking a thoughtful risk or simply acting without preparation?
  • What could I learn by allowing myself to be a beginner?

Reflection: The Fool reminds you that every journey begins somewhere. You do not need to know the entire path before taking the first step.

I

The Magician

Upright: Skill, confidence, action, creativity and using available resources.

Reversed: Manipulation, untapped ability, lack of direction or poor use of talents.

The Magician reminds you that you may already have the tools needed to move forward.

The Magician in a Reading

The Magician can appear when you have more ability, knowledge or resources available to you than you realise. It is a card of turning potential into action and using what is already within reach.

It can suggest confidence, creativity and deliberate action. Rather than waiting for the perfect circumstances, The Magician encourages you to recognise what you have and consider how you can use it effectively.

The Magician as Advice

Look at the resources already available to you. Your next step may not require something completely new; it may require you to make better use of what you already know, have or can do.

Questions for Reflection

  • What skills or resources do I already have?
  • Where could I take more deliberate action?
  • Am I recognising my own abilities clearly?
  • How could I turn an idea into something practical?

Reflection: The Magician reminds you that potential becomes meaningful through action. You may already possess more of the tools needed for the next step than you realise.

II

The High Priestess

Upright: Intuition, inner wisdom, mystery and hidden knowledge.

Reversed: Ignoring intuition, confusion, secrecy or emotional disconnection.

The High Priestess encourages patience and careful listening to your inner voice.

The High Priestess in a Reading

The High Priestess often appears when something deserves patience rather than an immediate answer. It can point towards intuition, observation and information that has not yet become fully visible.

Instead of encouraging you to force a decision, this card can suggest stepping back and paying attention to what you already sense. Sometimes understanding develops through quiet observation rather than immediate action.

The High Priestess as Advice

Give yourself time to listen before acting. Pay attention to subtle feelings, patterns and observations that you may have overlooked. Not every answer needs to be discovered immediately.

Questions for Reflection

  • What is my intuition telling me?
  • Am I giving myself enough time to understand this situation?
  • Is there something I already sense but am avoiding?
  • What might become clearer if I stopped trying to force an answer?

Reflection: The High Priestess reminds you that understanding does not always arrive through action. Sometimes the most useful step is to pause, observe and listen.

III

The Empress

Upright: Creativity, abundance, nurturing, comfort and growth.

Reversed: Creative blocks, dependence, neglecting yourself or giving too much.

The Empress is connected with care, creation and allowing something meaningful to grow.

The Empress in a Reading

The Empress often appears when something in your life needs patience, care and attention. It can represent creativity, comfort, growth and the gradual development of something that matters to you.

This card can also encourage you to notice what is already flourishing. Growth does not always happen through force or constant effort. Sometimes giving something the right environment is what allows it to develop naturally.

The Empress as Advice

Give yourself and the things that matter to you room to grow. Consider where more patience, creativity or care could make a meaningful difference. At the same time, remember that caring for others should not require you to ignore your own needs.

Questions for Reflection

  • What in my life deserves more care and attention?
  • What am I currently trying to grow?
  • Where could I allow more creativity into my life?
  • Am I giving too much to others while neglecting myself?

Reflection: The Empress reminds you that meaningful growth often needs patience, care and a supportive environment. Look at what you are nurturing and consider whether it is receiving what it needs to flourish.

IV

The Emperor

Upright: Structure, stability, leadership, authority and discipline.

Reversed: Rigidity, control, stubbornness or misuse of authority.

The Emperor suggests creating order, setting boundaries and taking responsibility.

The Emperor in a Reading

The Emperor often appears when structure and stability are important. It can point towards responsibility, organisation, leadership and the need to establish clear boundaries.

Rather than simply representing authority over others, the Emperor can also represent becoming more disciplined in your own life. It may ask you to decide what needs to be organised, protected or brought under greater control.

The Emperor as Advice

Create structure where it would genuinely help you. Set clear boundaries, make practical decisions and take responsibility for what is within your control.

At the same time, consider whether your need for order has become excessive. Stability should provide support rather than prevent flexibility and growth.

Questions for Reflection

  • Where would greater structure help me?
  • What boundaries do I need to establish?
  • What responsibilities am I ready to take ownership of?
  • Am I creating stability, or am I trying to control everything?

Reflection: The Emperor reminds you that stability can come from clear boundaries, responsibility and deliberate action. Consider where stronger foundations could help you move forward with confidence.

V

The Hierophant

Upright: Tradition, learning, guidance, shared values and established systems.

Reversed: Challenging tradition, restrictive beliefs or following your own path.

The Hierophant can represent teachers, institutions or beliefs that shape your understanding.

The Hierophant in a Reading

The Hierophant often appears when established knowledge, traditions or guidance have an important role in a situation. It can represent learning from someone with experience, following a recognised system or finding support within a community that shares your values.

The card can also encourage you to examine the beliefs you have inherited. Traditions can provide valuable structure, but they are not automatically right for every person or situation.

The Hierophant as Advice

Consider whether guidance, education or the experience of others could help you. A teacher, mentor or established method may provide a useful foundation for your next step.

At the same time, ask whether you are following a rule simply because you have always been told to follow it. Learning from tradition does not mean you have to surrender your own judgement.

Questions for Reflection

  • What can I learn from someone with more experience?
  • Which beliefs currently influence my decisions?
  • Am I following a tradition because it genuinely helps me?
  • Where might I benefit from questioning an old assumption?

Reflection: The Hierophant reminds you that knowledge can come through teachers, traditions and shared experience. Learn from what has come before, while remaining willing to decide what genuinely belongs on your own path.

VI

The Lovers

Upright: Connection, harmony, values, attraction and meaningful choices.

Reversed: Disharmony, imbalance, conflict or choices that do not match your values.

The Lovers is not only about romance. It can also represent alignment, trust and important decisions.

The Lovers in a Reading

The Lovers can appear when a meaningful relationship, important choice or question of personal values is at the centre of a situation. Although the card is often associated with romantic relationships, its meaning is much broader than romance.

At its heart, The Lovers can ask whether your actions are aligned with what you genuinely believe and value. It can also highlight the importance of honesty, communication and mutual respect within a relationship.

The Lovers as Advice

Be honest about what matters to you. When faced with an important choice, consider not only what seems easiest in the moment but whether the decision reflects the person you want to be.

In relationships, this card can encourage open communication and a willingness to understand another person's perspective without losing sight of your own values.

Questions for Reflection

  • What values are most important to me right now?
  • Does this choice reflect what I genuinely believe?
  • Where could more honest communication help?
  • Am I choosing from my own values or from outside pressure?

Reflection: The Lovers reminds you that meaningful choices are often about alignment. Consider whether your decisions, relationships and actions reflect what matters most to you.

VII

The Chariot

Upright: Determination, direction, progress, confidence and self-control.

Reversed: Lack of direction, aggression, obstacles or losing control.

The Chariot encourages focused action and steady movement toward a goal.

The Chariot in a Reading

The Chariot often appears when determination and focused effort are needed. It can represent moving forward despite competing pressures, obstacles or uncertainty.

The card is also connected with direction. Progress becomes easier when you know where you are trying to go and are willing to maintain control over the forces pulling you in different directions.

The Chariot as Advice

Choose your direction and commit to it. Focus your energy on what you can control rather than allowing distractions or conflicting priorities to pull you away from your goal.

Determination does not mean forcing everything to happen immediately. Be willing to adjust your approach while keeping sight of the destination.

Questions for Reflection

  • What direction am I trying to move in?
  • What is currently competing for my attention?
  • Where would greater self-control help me?
  • Am I moving forward with purpose or simply trying to escape where I am?

Reflection: The Chariot reminds you that progress often comes from combining determination with direction. Decide where you want to go, take control of what you can and keep moving with purpose.

VIII

Strength

Upright: Courage, patience, compassion, inner strength and calm confidence.

Reversed: Self-doubt, insecurity, impatience or emotional exhaustion.

Strength represents gentle courage rather than force. It suggests handling challenges with patience and self-control.

Strength in a Reading

Strength often appears when a situation requires courage, patience or emotional resilience. Rather than encouraging force, this card suggests that lasting strength can come from remaining calm and responding thoughtfully.

It can also represent compassion, particularly when dealing with difficult emotions or challenging circumstances. Strength is not about pretending that something is easy; it is about finding the confidence to face it without allowing fear or anger to take over.

Strength as Advice

Approach the situation with patience and confidence. You may have more inner resilience than you realise, and you do not necessarily need to prove your strength through confrontation.

If emotions are running high, give yourself time to respond rather than react. Calmness can be a form of strength in itself.

Questions for Reflection

  • Where am I being asked to show courage?
  • How could patience change the way I approach this situation?
  • Am I reacting from fear, anger or insecurity?
  • How can I be strong without becoming controlling?

Reflection: Strength reminds you that courage does not always look like force. Sometimes the strongest response is patience, compassion and the ability to remain steady when circumstances are difficult.

IX

The Hermit

Upright: Reflection, solitude, wisdom, guidance and inner discovery.

Reversed: Isolation, loneliness, withdrawal or avoiding self-reflection.

The Hermit suggests stepping back from outside noise to search for clarity within.

The Hermit in a Reading

The Hermit often appears when you need space to think, reflect or understand something more deeply. It can suggest stepping away from distractions and allowing yourself time to consider what you genuinely believe or want.

Solitude does not necessarily mean loneliness. The Hermit's quiet can be purposeful, giving you an opportunity to learn from your experiences and develop a clearer understanding of yourself.

The Hermit as Advice

Give yourself permission to slow down. You may not need another person's opinion immediately; some answers become clearer when you have enough quiet space to hear your own thoughts.

At the same time, remember that healthy solitude is different from completely withdrawing from the people who support you. Reflection should help you reconnect with yourself, not cut you off from others.

Questions for Reflection

  • What do I need time to think about?
  • Am I listening to my own thoughts or mainly to outside opinions?
  • What have my recent experiences taught me?
  • Am I taking useful time alone, or am I isolating myself?

Reflection: The Hermit reminds you that stepping back can create space for understanding. Sometimes clarity comes not from finding more information, but from giving yourself enough quiet to recognise what you already know.

X

Wheel of Fortune

Upright: Change, cycles, opportunity, movement and turning points.

Reversed: Resistance to change, setbacks, repeated patterns or feeling stuck.

The Wheel of Fortune reminds you that situations change and no phase lasts forever.

Wheel of Fortune in a Reading

The Wheel of Fortune often appears when circumstances are changing or when a significant turning point is approaching. It can represent movement, unexpected opportunities and the natural cycles of life.

Not every change is something you can control. The card can encourage you to recognise what is changing around you and consider how you can respond rather than trying to hold everything exactly as it was.

Wheel of Fortune as Advice

Stay open to change and pay attention to opportunities as they appear. If circumstances are shifting, consider what you can influence and what you simply need to allow to unfold.

If you are experiencing a difficult period, remember that circumstances can change again. A challenging phase does not necessarily define what comes next.

Questions for Reflection

  • What is currently changing in my life?
  • Am I resisting a change that may be necessary?
  • What opportunities could I be overlooking?
  • What is within my control, and what do I need to accept?

Reflection: The Wheel of Fortune reminds you that life moves through changing cycles. You cannot control every turn of the wheel, but you can choose how you respond when circumstances shift.

XI

Justice

Upright: Truth, fairness, accountability, balance and clear decisions.

Reversed: Dishonesty, unfairness, avoidance or refusing responsibility.

Justice asks you to look honestly at actions, consequences and the facts of a situation.

XII

The Hanged Man

Upright: Pause, surrender, patience, release and a new perspective.

Reversed: Delays, resistance, indecision or feeling trapped.

The Hanged Man suggests that progress may require stopping, letting go or seeing things differently.

XIII

Death

Upright: Endings, transformation, release, transition and renewal.

Reversed: Fear of change, resistance, stagnation or holding onto the past.

Death rarely refers to physical death. It usually represents the ending of one phase so another can begin.

XIV

Temperance

Upright: Balance, patience, healing, harmony and moderation.

Reversed: Excess, imbalance, impatience or lack of cooperation.

Temperance encourages a calm, measured approach and the blending of different ideas or energies.

XV

The Devil

Upright: Attachment, temptation, unhealthy habits, materialism and restriction.

Reversed: Breaking free, awareness, release and reclaiming control.

The Devil may highlight patterns, fears or attachments that limit your freedom.

XVI

The Tower

Upright: Sudden change, disruption, revelation and rebuilding.

Reversed: Avoiding change, fear of disruption or delaying an unavoidable truth.

The Tower can feel unsettling, but it often clears away something unstable so a stronger foundation can be built.

XVII

The Star

Upright: Hope, renewal, healing, inspiration and trust.

Reversed: Discouragement, doubt, lack of faith or emotional exhaustion.

The Star brings a sense of calm after difficulty and encourages hope for what comes next.

XVIII

The Moon

Upright: Intuition, uncertainty, dreams, illusion and hidden emotions.

Reversed: Confusion lifting, truth emerging, anxiety or emotional clarity.

The Moon suggests moving carefully when not everything is clear and paying attention to instinct.

XIX

The Sun

Upright: Joy, confidence, success, clarity and vitality.

Reversed: Delayed happiness, low confidence, unrealistic expectations or temporary setbacks.

The Sun is associated with warmth, openness and a clearer understanding of the path ahead.

XX

Judgement

Upright: Awakening, reflection, renewal, accountability and important decisions.

Reversed: Self-doubt, avoidance, harsh judgement or refusing to learn from the past.

Judgement encourages honest reflection and the chance to move forward with greater awareness.

XXI

The World

Upright: Completion, achievement, fulfilment, wholeness and successful endings.

Reversed: Unfinished business, delays, lack of closure or a goal still out of reach.

The World represents the completion of a cycle and the understanding gained through the journey.

The Fool's Journey

The Major Arcana can be viewed as a symbolic journey from innocence and possibility in The Fool to completion and understanding in The World. Each card represents a stage of growth, challenge or discovery along the way.

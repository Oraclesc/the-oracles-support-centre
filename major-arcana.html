Guides / Major Arcana

The Major Arcana Tarot Cards

The Major Arcana is made up of 22 cards that represent important lessons, turning points, personal growth and powerful life experiences. Together, they tell a symbolic story often known as The Fool's Journey.

0

The Fool

Upright: New beginnings, freedom, curiosity, adventure and taking a leap of faith.

Reversed: Recklessness, hesitation, poor planning or fear of beginning something new.

The Fool represents the beginning of a journey. It encourages openness, trust and a willingness to step into the unknown.

The Fool in a Reading

The Fool can appear when you are standing at the beginning of something unfamiliar. It may suggest curiosity, possibility and the willingness to learn through experience rather than knowing every answer beforehand.

The card does not necessarily mean that you should act without thinking. Its invitation to take a leap of faith can be balanced by awareness of the risks involved and a willingness to learn from what happens next.

The Fool as Advice

Consider where you may be holding yourself back simply because you cannot predict the outcome. The Fool can encourage you to approach a new experience with curiosity while still using sensible judgement.

Questions for Reflection

  • What new experience am I being invited to explore?
  • Where could I benefit from being more open-minded?
  • Am I taking a thoughtful risk or simply acting without preparation?
  • What could I learn by allowing myself to be a beginner?

Reflection: The Fool reminds you that every journey begins somewhere. You do not need to know the entire path before taking the first step.

I

The Magician

Upright: Skill, confidence, action, creativity and using available resources.

Reversed: Manipulation, untapped ability, lack of direction or poor use of talents.

The Magician reminds you that you may already have the tools needed to move forward.

The Magician in a Reading

The Magician can appear when you have more ability, knowledge or resources available to you than you realise. It is a card of turning potential into action and using what is already within reach.

It can suggest confidence, creativity and deliberate action. Rather than waiting for the perfect circumstances, The Magician encourages you to recognise what you have and consider how you can use it effectively.

The Magician as Advice

Look at the resources already available to you. Your next step may not require something completely new; it may require you to make better use of what you already know, have or can do.

Questions for Reflection

  • What skills or resources do I already have?
  • Where could I take more deliberate action?
  • Am I recognising my own abilities clearly?
  • How could I turn an idea into something practical?

Reflection: The Magician reminds you that potential becomes meaningful through action. You may already possess more of the tools needed for the next step than you realise.

II

The High Priestess

Upright: Intuition, inner wisdom, mystery and hidden knowledge.

Reversed: Ignoring intuition, confusion, secrecy or emotional disconnection.

The High Priestess encourages patience and careful listening to your inner voice.

The High Priestess in a Reading

The High Priestess often appears when something deserves patience rather than an immediate answer. It can point towards intuition, observation and information that has not yet become fully visible.

Instead of encouraging you to force a decision, this card can suggest stepping back and paying attention to what you already sense. Sometimes understanding develops through quiet observation rather than immediate action.

The High Priestess as Advice

Give yourself time to listen before acting. Pay attention to subtle feelings, patterns and observations that you may have overlooked. Not every answer needs to be discovered immediately.

Questions for Reflection

  • What is my intuition telling me?
  • Am I giving myself enough time to understand this situation?
  • Is there something I already sense but am avoiding?
  • What might become clearer if I stopped trying to force an answer?

Reflection: The High Priestess reminds you that understanding does not always arrive through action. Sometimes the most useful step is to pause, observe and listen.

III

The Empress

Upright: Creativity, abundance, nurturing, comfort and growth.

Reversed: Creative blocks, dependence, neglecting yourself or giving too much.

The Empress is connected with care, creation and allowing something meaningful to grow.

The Empress in a Reading

The Empress often appears when something in your life needs patience, care and attention. It can represent creativity, comfort, growth and the gradual development of something that matters to you.

This card can also encourage you to notice what is already flourishing. Growth does not always happen through force or constant effort. Sometimes giving something the right environment is what allows it to develop naturally.

The Empress as Advice

Give yourself and the things that matter to you room to grow. Consider where more patience, creativity or care could make a meaningful difference. At the same time, remember that caring for others should not require you to ignore your own needs.

Questions for Reflection

  • What in my life deserves more care and attention?
  • What am I currently trying to grow?
  • Where could I allow more creativity into my life?
  • Am I giving too much to others while neglecting myself?

Reflection: The Empress reminds you that meaningful growth often needs patience, care and a supportive environment. Look at what you are nurturing and consider whether it is receiving what it needs to flourish.

IV

The Emperor

Upright: Structure, stability, leadership, authority and discipline.

Reversed: Rigidity, control, stubbornness or misuse of authority.

The Emperor suggests creating order, setting boundaries and taking responsibility.

The Emperor in a Reading

The Emperor often appears when structure and stability are important. It can point towards responsibility, organisation, leadership and the need to establish clear boundaries.

Rather than simply representing authority over others, the Emperor can also represent becoming more disciplined in your own life. It may ask you to decide what needs to be organised, protected or brought under greater control.

The Emperor as Advice

Create structure where it would genuinely help you. Set clear boundaries, make practical decisions and take responsibility for what is within your control. At the same time, consider whether your need for order has become excessive.

Questions for Reflection

  • Where would greater structure help me?
  • What boundaries do I need to establish?
  • What responsibilities am I ready to take ownership of?
  • Am I creating stability, or am I trying to control everything?

Reflection: The Emperor reminds you that stability can come from clear boundaries, responsibility and deliberate action.

V

The Hierophant

Upright: Tradition, teaching, guidance, shared beliefs and established structures.

Reversed: Questioning tradition, unconventional thinking, restriction or following rules without understanding them.

The Hierophant brings attention to knowledge, tradition and the systems that shape how we understand something.

The Hierophant in a Reading

The Hierophant can represent learning from someone with experience, joining an established community or finding value in practices that have been passed down over time.

It can also ask you to examine the beliefs you have inherited. A tradition can provide useful structure, but it does not necessarily need to be followed simply because it has always been followed.

The Hierophant as Advice

Look for guidance where it is genuinely useful. Learn from people with experience, but allow yourself to think critically about what you are being taught.

Questions for Reflection

  • What could I learn from someone with more experience?
  • Which beliefs are genuinely my own?
  • Am I following a rule because it is useful or simply because it is familiar?
  • Where could structure help me?

Reflection: The Hierophant reminds you that knowledge can be gained through teachers, traditions and shared experience, while still leaving room to form your own understanding.

VI

The Lovers

Upright: Connection, relationships, harmony, values, choices and alignment.

Reversed: Disharmony, conflicting values, difficult choices or disconnection.

The Lovers can represent meaningful connection and choices that require honesty about what matters to you.

The Lovers in a Reading

The Lovers is often associated with relationships and meaningful connections, but its message can extend beyond romance. It can represent an important choice and the need to consider whether your actions are aligned with your deeper values.

In a relationship reading, it may highlight connection, mutual understanding and honest communication. In other situations, it can ask whether the path you are choosing genuinely reflects what matters to you.

The Lovers as Advice

Be honest about what you value. Consider not only what appears easiest in the moment but whether the decision reflects the person you want to be.

Questions for Reflection

  • What matters most to me in this situation?
  • Are my actions aligned with my values?
  • Is this connection based on honesty and mutual respect?
  • What choice would feel most authentic to me?

Reflection: The Lovers can remind you that meaningful choices are often about alignment between your values, actions and relationships.

VII

The Chariot

Upright: Determination, direction, confidence, progress and self-control.

Reversed: Loss of direction, frustration, scattered energy or trying to force progress.

The Chariot encourages purposeful movement and bringing competing priorities into a clearer direction.

The Chariot in a Reading

The Chariot appears when determination and direction are important. It can represent moving forward despite obstacles, particularly when you have a clear idea of where you want to go.

The card can also highlight competing forces. Progress may require you to bring different priorities under control rather than allowing each one to pull you in a different direction.

The Chariot as Advice

Choose your direction and commit to it. You cannot control every circumstance around you, but you can decide how you respond to obstacles and where you put your energy.

Questions for Reflection

  • Where am I trying to go?
  • What is currently competing for my attention?
  • What can I actually control?
  • Am I moving forward with purpose or simply trying to force an outcome?

Reflection: The Chariot encourages purposeful movement. Progress becomes easier when determination is supported by direction and self-control.

VIII

Strength

Upright: Courage, patience, compassion, resilience and inner confidence.

Reversed: Self-doubt, insecurity, frustration or difficulty managing strong emotions.

Strength is less about physical power than the ability to respond calmly when circumstances are challenging.

Strength in a Reading

Strength can represent patience, emotional resilience and the confidence to handle difficulty without immediately reacting.

The card may appear when gentleness is more effective than force. Sometimes strength means knowing when to remain calm, listen or give a situation time to develop.

Strength as Advice

Trust your ability to handle the situation without needing to dominate it. Give yourself time to respond rather than reacting immediately.

Questions for Reflection

  • What would a calm response look like here?
  • Where could patience help me?
  • Am I reacting from fear or responding from confidence?
  • What strength have I already shown in this situation?

Reflection: Strength reminds you that resilience can be quiet. Patience, compassion and emotional control can sometimes achieve more than force.

IX

The Hermit

Upright: Solitude, reflection, inner wisdom, searching and personal understanding.

Reversed: Isolation, withdrawal, loneliness or avoiding necessary connection.

The Hermit encourages you to step back from outside noise and consider what you genuinely think and feel.

The Hermit in a Reading

The Hermit can represent a period of learning, reflection or deliberately spending time alone to gain a clearer understanding.

Solitude can be useful, but the card does not necessarily suggest withdrawing from everyone. Sometimes reflection helps you understand what kind of connection or support you actually need.

The Hermit as Advice

Give yourself permission to slow down. If you have been surrounded by other people's opinions, create some space to hear your own. Reading, learning, journaling or quiet thought may help you see the situation differently.

Questions for Reflection

  • What do I really think when outside opinions are removed?
  • Would some quiet time help me understand this situation?
  • Am I choosing healthy solitude or avoiding people?
  • What have I learned from my recent experiences?

Reflection: The Hermit reminds you that stepping back can create clarity. Sometimes the next useful answer begins with giving yourself enough quiet to hear your own thoughts.

X

Wheel of Fortune

Upright: Change, cycles, opportunity, movement and shifting circumstances.

Reversed: Resistance to change, setbacks, repeating patterns or feeling stuck.

The Wheel of Fortune represents movement and the changing circumstances that shape our lives.

Wheel of Fortune in a Reading

The Wheel of Fortune can appear when circumstances are changing or when a situation is entering a different phase. It can remind you that not every part of life can be controlled.

Its cycles can also encourage you to notice patterns. Understanding what has happened before may help you recognise what is changing now.

Wheel of Fortune as Advice

Stay adaptable. Focus on what you can influence while accepting that some circumstances will change regardless of your plans.

Questions for Reflection

  • What is changing around me?
  • Which parts of this situation are within my control?
  • Am I repeating a pattern I have seen before?
  • How could I respond more flexibly?

Reflection: The Wheel of Fortune reminds you that circumstances move in cycles. Adaptability can help you navigate change without needing to control every outcome.

XI

Justice

Upright: Fairness, truth, accountability, balance and clear decisions.

Reversed: Bias, avoidance, unfairness or difficulty accepting consequences.

Justice brings attention to honesty, responsibility and the consequences of decisions.

Justice in a Reading

Justice may appear when facts, fairness or accountability need careful attention. It encourages you to look at a situation as honestly as possible, including your own part in it.

The card does not promise that every outcome will feel fair. Instead, it asks you to consider what is true, what is within your responsibility and what evidence should guide your decision.

Justice as Advice

Separate facts from assumptions. Consider different perspectives and take responsibility for the choices that belong to you.

Questions for Reflection

  • What facts do I actually know?
  • Am I looking at this situation fairly?
  • What part of this situation is my responsibility?
  • What decision would I respect later?

Reflection: Justice encourages honesty and accountability. Clear decisions become easier when you are willing to see the situation without deliberately avoiding uncomfortable truths.

XII

The Hanged Man

Upright: Pause, surrender, perspective, patience and letting go.

Reversed: Stagnation, resistance, delay or difficulty seeing a different perspective.

The Hanged Man invites you to pause and consider whether a different perspective could change your understanding.

The Hanged Man in a Reading

This card can appear when pushing harder is not producing useful progress. A pause may create space to reconsider assumptions or see a familiar situation differently.

It can also suggest accepting that some things cannot be rushed. A period of uncertainty does not necessarily mean that nothing is happening.

The Hanged Man as Advice

Pause before forcing a solution. Ask whether there is another way to understand the situation and whether something you are holding tightly needs to be released.

Questions for Reflection

  • What perspective am I missing?
  • What would happen if I stopped trying to force an outcome?
  • What am I finding difficult to let go of?
  • Could a temporary pause be useful?

Reflection: The Hanged Man reminds you that a change in perspective can sometimes achieve what effort alone cannot.

XIII

Death

Upright: Transformation, endings, release, transition and renewal.

Reversed: Resistance to change, fear of endings, stagnation or holding onto the past.

Death represents transformation rather than a literal prediction of physical death.

Death in a Reading

Death often appears when an old phase is ending and something needs to change. The process can feel uncomfortable because endings create space before the next stage becomes clear.

The card can relate to habits, relationships, identities, plans or ways of thinking that have reached the end of their usefulness.

Death as Advice

Consider what you may need to release. An ending does not automatically mean failure; sometimes letting something finish is what allows a different chapter to begin.

Questions for Reflection

  • What has reached the end of its usefulness?
  • What am I afraid to release?
  • What space could an ending create?
  • What might I need to allow to change?

Reflection: Death reminds you that change is part of growth. Releasing what no longer fits can create room for something new.

XIV

Temperance

Upright: Balance, patience, moderation, harmony and integration.

Reversed: Imbalance, excess, impatience or difficulty finding a middle ground.

Temperance encourages a measured approach and the ability to bring different elements into balance.

Temperance in a Reading

Temperance can appear when a situation benefits from patience and gradual adjustment. Rather than choosing between extremes, you may find a better result by combining different needs or approaches.

The card can also suggest allowing progress to happen at a sustainable pace rather than demanding immediate results.

Temperance as Advice

Look for the middle ground. Give yourself time to adjust and avoid making decisions purely from extremes of emotion or impatience.

Questions for Reflection

  • Where is my life currently out of balance?
  • What could I combine rather than choose between?
  • Am I allowing enough time for things to develop?
  • Where would moderation help?

Reflection: Temperance reminds you that balance is often created gradually. Small adjustments can produce a more sustainable direction.

XV

The Devil

Upright: Attachment, temptation, unhealthy patterns, material desire and feeling trapped.

Reversed: Awareness, release, breaking patterns or recognising unhealthy attachments.

The Devil brings attention to patterns or attachments that may be limiting your sense of freedom.

The Devil in a Reading

The card can highlight habits, fears, desires or relationships that have gained more influence than you intended. It does not necessarily mean that something is permanently trapping you.

Sometimes the most important step is recognising where you still have a choice.

The Devil as Advice

Look honestly at what has power over your decisions. Avoid judging yourself for having difficult patterns; focus instead on understanding them and identifying where change is possible.

Questions for Reflection

  • What pattern do I feel stuck inside?
  • What am I afraid to change?
  • Where might I have more choice than I realise?
  • What attachment is no longer helping me?

Reflection: The Devil encourages honest awareness. Recognising an unhealthy pattern is often the first step towards changing it.

XVI

The Tower

Upright: Upheaval, revelation, disruption, sudden change and breaking unstable foundations.

Reversed: Resistance to change, delayed upheaval, fear of disruption or internal transformation.

The Tower represents moments when an existing structure can no longer continue in the same way.

The Tower in a Reading

The Tower can feel uncomfortable because it is associated with disruption and revelations. It may indicate that an assumption, plan or structure needs to be reconsidered.

Although change can be unsettling, the card can also expose something that was already unstable and create an opportunity to rebuild more honestly.

The Tower as Advice

Do not automatically cling to something simply because it is familiar. If new information challenges an old assumption, give yourself permission to reassess.

Questions for Reflection

  • What assumption is being challenged?
  • What foundation no longer feels stable?
  • What truth am I finding difficult to acknowledge?
  • What could be rebuilt differently?

Reflection: The Tower reminds you that disruption can reveal what needs to change. Not every ending is something that needs to be prevented.

XVII

The Star

Upright: Hope, renewal, healing, inspiration and clarity.

Reversed: Discouragement, loss of confidence, doubt or difficulty recognising hope.

The Star represents hope and the possibility of moving forward with renewed perspective.

The Star in a Reading

The Star can appear after a difficult period when you are beginning to reconnect with optimism. It may represent healing, inspiration or a clearer sense of what you want to work towards.

Hope does not require pretending that difficulties never happened. It can mean recognising that difficulty does not have to define everything that comes next.

The Star as Advice

Give yourself permission to imagine a better direction. Focus on what you can nurture and allow optimism to return gradually rather than demanding that you feel positive immediately.

Questions for Reflection

  • What gives me a genuine sense of hope?
  • What am I beginning to recover from?
  • What future would I like to work towards?
  • Where could I allow more optimism?

Reflection: The Star reminds you that hope can return gradually. Look for the direction that feels meaningful and take the next manageable step towards it.

XVIII

The Moon

Upright: Uncertainty, intuition, dreams, imagination and hidden fears.

Reversed: Confusion clearing, anxiety, distorted perceptions or avoiding uncertainty.

The Moon represents situations where everything may not be as clear as it first appears.

The Moon in a Reading

The Moon can appear when emotions, uncertainty or incomplete information make a situation difficult to understand. It encourages you to distinguish between what you know and what you fear might be true.

Intuition can be useful here, but so can patience and checking facts. Strong feelings are meaningful without necessarily being proof of an outcome.

The Moon as Advice

Move carefully when information is incomplete. Avoid making major assumptions based solely on fear or wishful thinking. Give yourself time for the situation to become clearer.

Questions for Reflection

  • What do I actually know?
  • Which parts of this situation are assumptions?
  • Could fear be influencing my interpretation?
  • What information would help me feel clearer?

Reflection: The Moon reminds you that uncertainty can distort perception. Slow down, check what you know and allow clarity to develop.

XIX

The Sun

Upright: Joy, clarity, confidence, success, vitality and optimism.

Reversed: Temporary disappointment, lack of confidence, delayed success or difficulty recognising the positive.

The Sun represents clarity, warmth and the confidence that comes from being able to see a situation more clearly.

The Sun in a Reading

The Sun often appears when circumstances are becoming clearer or when there is an opportunity to experience greater optimism and confidence. It can represent success, happiness and a renewed sense of energy.

The card can also encourage openness and honesty. When things are brought into the light, misunderstandings can become easier to resolve and you may feel more comfortable being yourself.

The Sun as Advice

Look for what is working and allow yourself to acknowledge your progress. Be open about what you want and let yourself approach the situation with confidence rather than automatically expecting the worst.

If you have recently experienced difficulty, remember that recovery does not require everything to become perfect immediately. Look for the genuine positives that are already present.

Questions for Reflection

  • What is going well that I may be overlooking?
  • Where could I allow myself to feel more confident?
  • What would greater clarity help me understand?
  • How can I bring more openness and optimism into this situation?

Reflection: The Sun reminds you to recognise clarity, progress and genuine reasons for optimism. Allow yourself to acknowledge what is going well while continuing to move forward with confidence.

XX

Judgement

Upright: Awakening, reflection, renewal, accountability and important decisions.

Reversed: Self-doubt, avoidance, harsh judgement or refusing to learn from the past.

Judgement encourages honest reflection and the chance to move forward with greater awareness.

Judgement in a Reading

Judgement can appear when an experience is asking to be understood rather than simply repeated. It may encourage you to look honestly at the past, recognise what you have learned and decide what you want to carry forward.

The card can also represent an important decision that benefits from self-awareness rather than guilt or harsh self-criticism.

Judgement as Advice

Review the situation honestly. Acknowledge mistakes without allowing them to define you, and recognise what your experiences have taught you.

Questions for Reflection

  • What have I learned from the past?
  • What am I ready to leave behind?
  • Where do I need to be more honest with myself?
  • What decision would help me move forward with greater awareness?

Reflection: Judgement reminds you that reflection can create renewal. Understanding the past can help you make more conscious choices about what comes next.

XXI

The World

Upright: Completion, achievement, fulfilment, wholeness and successful endings.

Reversed: Unfinished business, delays, lack of closure or a goal still out of reach.

The World represents the completion of a cycle and the understanding gained through the journey.

The World in a Reading

The World can appear when a significant stage is reaching completion. It may represent achievement, closure or recognition of how far you have travelled.

Completion does not always mean that everything is perfect. Sometimes it means that you have learned what you needed from a particular stage and are ready to move into another.

The World as Advice

Take time to recognise what you have accomplished. If something remains unfinished, identify what is genuinely needed for closure rather than rushing immediately into the next chapter.

Questions for Reflection

  • What have I completed or learned?
  • How far have I come?
  • Is there anything that still needs closure?
  • What would I like to carry into the next stage?

Reflection: The World reminds you to recognise completion and the understanding gained through the journey. An ending can also be the beginning of something new.

The Fool's Journey

The Major Arcana can be viewed as a symbolic journey from innocence and possibility in The Fool to completion and understanding in The World. Each card represents a stage of growth, challenge or discovery along the way.

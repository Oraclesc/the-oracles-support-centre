Tarot Glossary

Tarot has its own vocabulary. These definitions explain common terms in plain language.

1

Arcana

The two main groups of the Tarot deck: Major Arcana and Minor Arcana.

2

Major Arcana

The 22 numbered cards from The Fool through The World, often used for broad themes and transitions.

3

Minor Arcana

The 56 cards divided into Cups, Pentacles, Swords and Wands, often exploring everyday experiences.

4

Suit

One of the four Minor Arcana groups, each with recurring themes and imagery.

5

Court Card

Page, Knight, Queen and King cards. They may represent people, roles, qualities or approaches.

6

Querent

The person asking a question or receiving a reading.

7

Spread

A layout in which each card has a defined position or role.

8

Reversal

A card appearing upside down. Readers differ on whether reversals have distinct meanings.

9

Clarifier

An optional extra card used to explore another card or question in more detail.

10

Significator

A card deliberately chosen to represent a person, situation or theme.

11

Intuition

A reader's immediate impression or association with a card.

12

Symbolism

The images, objects, colours, numbers and settings that communicate themes.

13

Card Combination

The way two or more cards influence one another through position, contrast and shared themes.

14

Rider–Waite–Smith

A highly influential early-20th-century Tarot deck created by A. E. Waite and illustrated by Pamela Colman Smith.

15

Numerology

The use of number patterns as an additional interpretive layer.

16

Reflection Question

A question designed to encourage examination rather than demand a fixed prediction.

17

Tarot Journal

A record of questions, cards, observations and later reflections.

18

Pip Card

A numbered Minor Arcana card, usually Ace through Ten. In Marseille-style decks, pip cards are often read through number, suit and the arrangement of the suit emblems rather than a fully illustrated scene.

19

Elemental Correspondence

A symbolic association between suits and elements. A common modern framework links Cups with Water, Wands with Fire, Swords with Air and Pentacles with Earth. Historical and contemporary systems can differ, so treat this as one interpretive framework rather than a universal rule.

20

Spread Position

The role assigned to a card in a layout, such as situation, challenge, resource or next step. The position helps shape the interpretation; the same card can answer different questions in different positions.

21

Card Combination

An interpretation formed by considering two or more cards together. Look for agreement, contrast, sequence and how one card qualifies another rather than simply adding keywords.

22

Archetype

A recurring symbolic role or pattern, such as a teacher, ruler, seeker or transformation. Archetypes can help describe broad themes, but they should not be used to label a person as if a card defines their identity.

23

Elemental Dignity

A method some readers use to judge how cards influence one another through elemental relationships, neighbouring cards or spread positions. Methods vary between traditions and should be explained rather than assumed.

24

Intuitive Reading

An approach that begins with personal impressions of an image, then tests those impressions against traditional meanings and the question. Intuition is a starting point for reflection, not proof of hidden facts.

25

Reading Ethics

Principles that guide a responsible reading, including informed consent, confidentiality, honest uncertainty and avoiding fear-based claims. Tarot should not replace qualified professional advice.

26

Tarot de Marseille

A family of related historical Tarot patterns associated with French and European card-making traditions. It is not one single unchanging deck; designs and printing details vary between editions.

27

Rider–Waite–Smith

The name used here for the influential deck developed around 1909 by Arthur Edward Waite and illustrated by Pamela Colman Smith. Rider issued the deck around 1909–1910, and some museum catalogues date early printed examples to 1910. Its illustrated Minor Arcana scenes provide a different visual reading method from Marseille pip cards.

28

Clarifying Card

An optional additional card used to explore a specific point in a reading. Draw one only when it addresses a clear question; repeatedly drawing cards until a preferred answer appears can make a reading less clear.

29

Interpretation

The meaning a reader develops by combining the card's imagery, traditional themes, spread position, question and surrounding cards. More than one interpretation may be reasonable, and the reader should explain uncertainty.

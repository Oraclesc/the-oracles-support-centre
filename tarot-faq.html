Tarot FAQ

These answers cover common questions people encounter when learning the cards.

1

Do I need to be psychic?

No. Tarot can be studied as a symbolic system using traditional meanings, imagery, questions and observation.

2

Can Tarot predict the future?

Traditions differ. This site presents readings as reflective tools rather than guaranteed predictions.

3

What does a reversed card mean?

It may suggest blocked energy, an internal expression, delay, excess or another perspective. Some readers do not use reversals.

4

How many cards should I draw?

One card is useful for focused reflection and three cards provide more context. More cards do not automatically make a better reading.

5

Can I read Tarot for myself?

Yes. Writing your first interpretation before checking a guide can help reduce projection.

6

Should I repeat the same question?

Usually give the first reading time to settle. Repeating it immediately can make the process confusing.

7

What is a good beginner deck?

Choose imagery you can understand and enjoy. The Rider–Waite–Smith tradition is common in beginner resources.

8

Major vs Minor Arcana?

The Major Arcana has 22 cards; the Minor Arcana has 56 cards across four suits.

9

What do the suits represent?

A common framework connects Cups with emotions, Wands with energy, Swords with thought and communication, and Pentacles with practical matters.

10

What is a clarifier?

An optional extra card used to explore another card. Too many clarifiers can make a reading less clear.

11

Can Tarot tell me what someone thinks?

A card cannot verify another person's private thoughts. Focus on communication, observed behaviour and your own choices.

12

Does Death mean physical death?

In ordinary Tarot interpretation, Death is commonly associated with endings, transition and transformation.

13

Is The Devil always negative?

No. It can be explored through attachment, habit, temptation, power or feeling constrained.

14

Can I create my own spread?

Yes. Give each position one clear purpose and test it in your journal.

15

Can Tarot replace professional advice?

No. It should not replace qualified medical, legal, financial, mental health or other specialist advice.

16

What if a reading makes me anxious?

Put the cards aside and return to the real-world situation. Seek appropriate support if needed.

17

Does every card have one fixed meaning?

No. Question, position, imagery and surrounding cards all affect interpretation.

🌙 Moon & Tarot practice

Instead of treating lunar phases as guarantees, use them as a recurring journaling structure:

  1. New Moon: What intention deserves attention?
  2. Waxing: What am I developing?
  3. Full Moon: What has become clearer?
  4. Waning: What can I release, simplify or stop feeding?

Pair each phase with one card and compare your notes later. The value is in the review, not in assuming the phase causes a particular outcome.

The Four Zodiac Elements and Tarot Suits

Astrology groups the twelve zodiac signs into four elements. Tarot readers often compare these elements with the four Minor Arcana suits. This is a useful symbolic framework, not a universal rule: decks and interpretive schools may use different systems.

Fire: Aries, Leo and Sagittarius

Fire is commonly associated with action, enthusiasm, courage and creative momentum. In Tarot, it is often compared with the suit of Wands.

Earth: Taurus, Virgo and Capricorn

Earth is associated with material needs, stability, resources and practical effort. It is commonly compared with Pentacles.

Air: Gemini, Libra and Aquarius

Air is linked with thought, language, ideas and decision-making. It is commonly compared with Swords.

Water: Cancer, Scorpio and Pisces

Water is associated with emotion, intuition, relationships and inner experience. It is commonly compared with Cups.

How to Use Zodiac Correspondences in a Reading

Start with the card's usual meaning and the position it occupies in the spread. Only then consider whether an astrological association adds a useful layer. For example, an element can suggest whether a situation is being approached through action, practical concerns, analysis or emotion. It should not override the question or the details of the reading.

If several cards from one suit appear together, the repeated element may be a useful theme to notice. You might ask whether the reading is especially focused on feelings, practical matters, communication or motivation. Compare that observation with the actual circumstances rather than treating it as a fixed prediction.

Major Arcana and Astrology: A Note on Different Systems

The twelve sign-to-card pairings shown above are commonly used in modern esoteric Tarot systems, but they are not the only correspondences found in Tarot literature. Some systems also assign planetary and elemental associations to other Major Arcana cards. Historical decks were not all designed around one shared astrological scheme, so it is helpful to identify which tradition a source is using.

When learning a new deck, follow its guidebook or the creator's stated system consistently before mixing correspondences from different sources. A Simple Tarot and Zodiac Journaling Exercise

  1. Choose a question about a current theme, not a guaranteed future event.
  2. Draw one card and write down its ordinary meaning and the details you notice in the image.
  3. If a zodiac or elemental correspondence is relevant, record it as an additional association rather than the main answer.
  4. Write one practical reflection or question that follows from the card.
  5. Return to your notes later and consider which observations were genuinely useful.

This approach keeps astrology and Tarot as tools for structured reflection while leaving room for different traditions and personal interpretations.

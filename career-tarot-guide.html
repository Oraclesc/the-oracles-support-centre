Career Tarot Guide

Career readings can organise thoughts about work and choices, but should sit alongside practical research and evidence.

1

Start With the Real Situation

Write down the opportunity and the facts you know: deadlines, responsibilities, finances, location and qualifications.

2

Questions That Lead to Action

What should I understand? What preparation would help? What strength can I use? What factor am I overlooking? What should I investigate before changing jobs?

3

Four-Card Career Spread

Card 1: current position. Card 2: challenge. Card 3: opportunity worth investigating. Card 4: practical focus for the next step.

4

Separate Symbolism From Evidence

A card about change does not prove you should resign and a card about success does not guarantee a job offer. Research the real situation.

5

After the Reading

Write one thing the cards helped you notice and one fact you still need to investigate.

✦

Worked Example: Considering a Job Change

Question: “What should I investigate before deciding whether to change jobs?” This question leaves room to examine preparation and trade-offs rather than asking a card to predict whether an offer will arrive.

Current position — Five of Pentacles: One possible reading is that the present situation feels insecure or under-resourced. Check whether this reflects workload, pay, support, confidence or something else; do not assume the card proves that a workplace is harmful.

Resource — Three of Pentacles: Consider which skills, colleagues, references or training could help. Ask for specific feedback and identify evidence of your experience.

Direction — Two of Wands: Compare realistic options. Research the role, commute, responsibilities, contract and development prospects before making a commitment.

Practical focus — Justice: Read the terms carefully, compare the facts fairly and check what you would gain or give up. The cards can help organise the questions, but the decision depends on real information.

Before Acting on a Career Reading

  • Check salary, hours, benefits and contract terms.
  • Consider savings, notice periods and any financial commitments.
  • Compare the actual duties with your skills and longer-term goals.
  • Seek reliable advice where employment, tax or financial rules are involved.

Use the reading to identify what to investigate next. Avoid resigning, investing or taking on debt solely because of a card.
